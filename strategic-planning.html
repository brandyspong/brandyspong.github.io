02 — Strategic Planning

Designing for alignment across an interlocking system.

A case study in strengthening school improvement planning within a larger district, county and state accountability framework—connecting evidence, strategy, implementation and resources without losing sight of the people doing the work.

Context

School improvement planning does not happen at the school level alone. In a large urban Bay Area school district serving more than 30,000 students, each school plan operates within a larger district planning and accountability system, which in turn sits within county and state frameworks for school improvement.

The district also carries a long history of state and county oversight. As a result, alignment, fiscal integrity, documentation and statutory compliance are not theoretical considerations. The planning system must work for school communities while also standing up to multiple layers of external and internal review.

The Accountability Environment
01

State Framework

Statutory requirements, accountability expectations, funding rules, planning requirements and statewide measures of school performance.

02

County Oversight & Support

Review, technical assistance and an additional layer of accountability connecting state expectations with district implementation.

03

District Planning & Accountability

District goals, data systems, fiscal controls, program requirements and cross-functional processes that establish the operating environment for schools.

04

School Improvement Planning

School communities identify needs, examine evidence, choose strategies, allocate resources and implement improvements for students.

The challenge is not simply aligning four layers on paper. Information and decisions need to remain coherent as they move through the system—and each layer includes different people, responsibilities and forms of accountability.

The Challenge

Traditional planning processes can fragment the work: needs assessment in one place, performance data in another, strategies somewhere else and expenditures in a separate budget process.

That fragmentation makes it harder for school teams to see the logic of their own plans—and harder for district teams to determine whether resources, strategies and identified needs actually align.

The design challenge was therefore larger than improving a planning template. It was to strengthen the architecture connecting school-level decision making to a broader improvement and accountability system.

The design creates a visible line from evidence to action: what students need, how the need is measured, what the school intends to do about it and how resources support that strategy. Stable connections between these elements preserve traceability as information moves through planning and budgeting.

Accountability Is Built In
Fiscal

Do resources follow strategy?

Expenditures connect back to planned activities and strategies rather than existing as an independent budget list.

Programmatic

Does strategy follow evidence?

Activities can be traced to identified student needs, relevant measures and intended outcomes.

Statutory

Does the plan satisfy the larger framework?

School-level planning remains connected to district, county and state requirements and accountability structures.

Design Principles
01

Design from relationships, not sections

Structure the planning process around how needs, evidence, strategies and resources relate—not around the order of fields in a compliance document.

02

Make alignment visible

Give school and central teams a way to see whether the reasoning connecting data, strategy and spending holds together.

03

Preserve traceability

Use stable connections between planning elements so that decisions can be followed across needs assessment, implementation, budgeting and review.

04

Reduce unnecessary complexity

Keep the school-facing planning experience as usable as possible while maintaining the underlying structure required for district and external accountability.

05

Design for multiple users

A planning system must work for principals and school teams while also producing information usable by program, data, fiscal and accountability teams.

06

Treat compliance as architecture

Build required checks into the structure of the process rather than relying on a final compliance review to discover disconnected decisions.

My Role

My contribution has been to work across the boundaries between school planning, program requirements, data, accountability and fiscal implementation—helping translate a complex set of requirements into a planning architecture that is both more coherent and more usable.

This work is inherently collaborative. During my time in the department, the district's planning systems have strengthened substantially through the work of high-functioning teams across multiple disciplines. My role has included identifying system connections and gaps, contributing to process and tool design, translating requirements across teams, and helping turn shared ideas into operational structures.
